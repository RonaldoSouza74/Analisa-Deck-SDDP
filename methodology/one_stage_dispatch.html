One-Stage Dispatch: Basic Formulation

Problem Formulation

We present now in more detail the one-stage dispatch problem formulation for stage \(t\):

\[ \begin{array}{l l} \min & \text{ICF} + \text{FCF} \\ \text{subject to} & \text{operating constraints in that stage} \\ \end{array} \]

where \(\text{ICF}\) is the immediate cost function and \(\text{FCF}\) is the future cost function.

Objective Function

The objective of the stochastic dispatch problem is to minimize the sum of immediate and future operating costs. At each stage \(t\), the immediate cost function \(\text{ICF}\) is given by the sum of thermal costs plus penalties for operating constraint violations:

\[\text{ICF} = \sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{j=1}^J c(j) \cdot g_{tk}(j) + c_{\delta} \cdot \delta_{t}\]

where:

Symbol Description Unit
\(k\) indexes load block in the stage
\(K\) number of load blocks
\(j\) indexes thermal plants
\(J\) number of thermal plants
\(c(j)\) operating cost of plant \(j\) \(\$/MWh\)
\(g_{tk}(j)\) energy generation of thermal plant \(j\) in stage \(t\), block \(k\) \(MWh\)
\(c_{\delta}\) generic representation of operating constraint violation cost \(\$/\text{violation unit}\)
\(\delta_{t}\) violation amount in stage \(t\) \(\text{violation unit}\)

As seen in the Stochastic Dispatch section, at each stage \(t\), the future cost function depends on the storage levels at the end of stage \(t\) and past streamflow:

\[\text{FCF} = \alpha_{t+1}(v_{t+1},a_{t})\]

where:

Symbol Description Unit
\(v_{t+1}\) final storage vector at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(a_{t}\) lateral inflow vector at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)

Basic Operating Constraints

The one-stage dispatch problem is subject to a series of operating constraints, that are particular to the different agents included in the problem. These constraints are detailed in the different articles of the Mathematical Modeling section.

The constraints listed below are essential for the formulation of the one-stage dispatch problem. A short explanation for each one of them is available in this page.

  • Water balance equation
  • Battery balance equation
  • Load supply equation
  • Operating limits
    • Hydro storage limits
    • Turbined outflow limits
    • Hydro energy generation
    • Thermal generation limits
    • VRE generation limits
    • Battery charge and discharge
    • Battery storage limits

Hydro Constraints

All the operating constraints related to hydro plants and reservoirs are explained in detail here.

Water balance

The water balance equation relates reservoir storage to inflow and outflow volumes.

At each stage and for each hydro plant, the reservoir storage at the end of stage is equal to:

  • the storage at the beggining of the stage

  • minus outflow volumes, including:

    • turbined outflow
    • spilled outflow
    • irrigation
    • infiltration, which is a function of storage
    • evaporation, which is a function of storage

  • plus inflow volumes, including:

    • outflow from upstream plants
    • lateral inflow
\[ \begin{split} v_{t+1}(i) & = v_t(i) \\ & - \sum\limits_{k=1}^{K} \left\{ u_{tk}(i) + s_{tk}(i) \right\} - \phi_{t}(i) - \gamma_{i}\left( v_t(i) \right) - \epsilon_{i}\left( v_t(i) \right) \\ & + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_U(i)} \sum\limits_{k=1}^{K} u_{tk}(m) + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_S(i)} \sum\limits_{k=1}^{K} s_{tk}(m) + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_F(i)} \gamma_{m}\left( v_t(m) \right) \\ & + a_t(i) \end{split} \]

where:

Symbol Description Unit
\(v_{t}(i)\) storage at plant \(i\) at the beggining of stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(a_{t}(i)\) lateral inflow at plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(u_{tk}(i)\) turbined outflow of plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) and load block \(k\) \(hm^3\)
\(s_{tk}(i)\) spilled outflow of plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) and load block \(k\) \(hm^3\)
\(\phi_{t}(i)\) irrigation from plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(\gamma_{i}(v_{t}(i))\) infiltration of plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(\epsilon_{i}(v_{t}(i))\) evaporation of plant \(i\) at stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(\mathcal{I}_U(i)\) set of hydro plants which turbine to plant \(i\)
\(\mathcal{I}_S(i)\) set of hydro plants which spill to plant \(i\)
\(\mathcal{I}_F(i)\) set of hydro plants which infiltrate to plant \(i\)

Storage limits

At each stage \(t\), the storage \(v_t(i)\) of hydro plant \(i\) is limited by a minimum storage \(\underline{v}_{t}(i)\) and a maximum storage \(\overline{v}_{t}(i)\).

\[\underline{v}_{t}(i) \leq v_{t}(i) \leq \overline{v}_{t}(i) \hspace{1cm} \]

Hydro energy production

Each hydro plant \(i\) is associated with a production factor function \(\rho_i\).

At each stage \(t\) and each load block \(k\), the energy generation \(g_{tk}(i)\) of a hydro plant \(i\) is defined as:

\[g_{tk}(i) = \rho_i (v_t(i)) \cdot u_{tk}(i) \]

Minimum and maximum turbined outflows

At each stage \(t\) and each load block \(k\), the turbined outflow \(u_{tk}(i)\) and its corresponding penalty variable \(\Delta u_{tk}(i)\) (included in the overall objective function) must be greater than a minimum turbined outflow limit \(\underline{u}_{tk}(i)\).

\[ \underline{u}_{tk}(i) \leq u_{tk}(i) + \Delta u_{tk}(i) \]

The maximum turbined outflow constraint is formulated as follows:

\[u_{tk}(i) \leq \min\left(\overline{u}_{tk}(i), \frac{\overline{g}_{tk}(i)}{\rho(v_{t}(i))}\right) \]

Thermal Constraints

Thermal generation limits

At each stage \(t\) and each load block \(k\), the generation of a thermal plant \(g_{tk}(j)\) is limited by a minimum generation \(\underline{g}_{tk}(j)\) and a maximum generation \(\overline{g}_{tk}(j)\).

\[\underline{g}_{tk}(j) \leq g_{tk}(j) \leq \overline{g}_{tk}(j) \]

VRE energy production

At each stage \(t\) and each load block \(k\), the available production \(p_{tk}(r)\) of a Variable Renewable Energy source is equal to its generation \(g_{tk}(r)\) plus its spillage \(q_{tk}(r)\).

\[g_{tk}(r) + q_{tk}(r) = p_{tk}(r) \]

Battery Constraints

Maximum battery discharge and charge

At each stage \(t\) and load block \(k\), the discharge \(ds_k(b)\) and the charge \(cr_k(b)\) of a battery \(b\) are limited by the capacity \(\overline{p}(b)\) of the battery.

\[ds_{tk}(b) \leq \overline{p}(b) \]
\[cr_{tk}(b) \leq \overline{p}(b) \]

Battery storage

At each stage \(t\), the storage \(vb_{t+1}(b)\) of a battery \(b\) is limited.

\[\underline{vb}_{t}(b) \leq vb_{t}(b) \leq \overline{vb}_{t}(b) \]

Battery balance

The energy balance equation of a battery is defined such that:

\[vb_{t+1}(b) = vb_{t}(b) + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left( \beta_{c}cr_{tk}(b) - ds_{tk}(b) \right) \]

where \(\beta_{c}\) is the charge efficiency of the battery.

Load Supply

At each stage \(t\) and each load block \(k\), the load supply constraint sums up the generation of all elements in the system and compares it to the energy demand.

\[\sum_{i=1}^I g_{tk}(i) + \sum_{j=1}^J g_{tk}(j) + \sum_{r=1}^R g_{tk}(r) + \sum_{b=1}^B \left( \beta_d(b) \cdot ds_k(b) - cr_k(b) \right) = d_{tk} \]

where \(\beta_d\) is the discharge efficiency of the battery and \(d_{tk}\) is the energy demand in stage \(t\), block \(k\).

Future Cost Function

As seen in the Dual DP section, the future cost function is represented by a set of linear constraints, each one representing a segment of the piecewise linear function.

\[\alpha_{t+1}(v_{t+1},\alpha_{t})=\alpha\]

For each segment \(p\) of the FCF there is the following corresponding constraint:

subject to:

\[ \begin{align*} &\alpha \geq w_t(p) + \sum_{i=1}^{I} \lambda_{tv}(i,p) \cdot v_{t+1}(i) + \sum_{i=1}^{I} \lambda_{ta}(i,p) \cdot a_t(i) \\ \end{align*} \]

where:

Symbol Description Unit
\(\alpha\) scalar variable that represents expected future operating cost \(\$\)
\(w_{t}(p)\) constant term of \(p^{th}\) segment \(\$\)
\(v_{t+1}(i)\) final storage of plant \(i\) in stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(\lambda_{tv}(i,p)\) final storage coefficient of plant \(i\) in the \(p^{th}\) segment \(\$/hm^3\)
\(a_t(i)\) lateral inflow of plant \(i\) in stage \(t\) \(hm^3\)
\(\lambda_{ta}(i,p)\) lateral inflow coefficient of plant \(i\) in the \(p^{th}\) segment \(\$/hm^3\)
