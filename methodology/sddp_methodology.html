SDDP Algorithm

Stochastic Dual Dynamic Programming Algorithm

Problem Formulation

The multistage stochastic hydrothermal dispatch is formulated as a stochastic dynamic programming problem, characterized by the following recursive equation:

\[ \begin{array}{l l l l l l} \tag{A.1} & \alpha(v_{t-1}, a_{t-1}) = \mathbb{E} \left[ \min z_t(e_t) + \alpha_{t+1}(v_t,a_t) \right] \\ \text{s.t} \\ & v_t(i) + s_t(i) + u_t(i) \\ & \; \; - \sum\limits_{m \in M_i} [s_t(m) + u_t(m)] = v_{t-1}(i) + a_t(i) \\ & v_t(i) \leq \overline{v}_t(i) \\ & u_t(i) \leq \overline{u}_t(i) \\ & e_t(i) = \rho_{i} \cdot u_t(i) \\ \end{array} \]

where \(i\) indexes the hydro plants, \(I\) denotes the number of hydro plants and \(z_t(e_t)\) represents the operating cost associated to the hydro generation \(e_t\), that is:

\[ \begin{array}{l l l l l l} \tag{A.2} z_t(e_t) & = & \min & \sum\limits_{j=1}^J c_{j} \cdot g_t(j) + c_{\delta} \delta_t \\ & & \text{s.t.} \\ & & & \sum\limits_{j=1}^{J} g_t(j) + \sum\limits_{i=1}^{I} e_t(j) + \delta_t = d_t \\ & & & g_t(j) \leq \overline{g}_t(j) \\ \end{array} \]

where \(j\) indexes the thermal plants and \(J\) is the number of thermal plants.

In principle, the recursive procedure \(\text{(A.1)}\) could be solved through a stochastic dynamic programming algorithm (SDP). However, the computational effort of the traditional SDP algorithm grows exponentially with the number of state variables in the problem.

Because of these limitations, the stochastic dual dynamic programming technique (SDDP) is used. SDDP produces the same results of traditional SDP, without the need to discretize the state space. The SDDP algorithm is an iterative procedure for constructing an approximation of the future cost function. Its accuracy depends on two parameters: the number of sampled states, denoted by \(L\), and the number of conditioned inflow scenarios used in the future cost function calculation, denoted by \( N. \)

SDDP Algorithm Steps

Selection of the Initial Set of States

The first iteration requires \(L\) initial states. For each stage \(t\) and state \(l\), \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) represents the initial conditions of storage and inflows.

Initial storage states

The initial storage state for stage for \(t = 1\) is a known value, equal to \(v_n\) for each of the \(L\) states. The initial storage states \({v_{t-1}^l},l=1,\ldots,L\) and , \(t = 2, \ldots, T\) are obtained dividing the reservoir capacity into \(L\) values. For example, if \(L = 5\), the storage states would be \(100\%\), \(75\%\), \(50\%\), \(25\%\) and \(0\%\).

Initial inflow states

At each stage \(t\), the initial hydrological condition \(a_n\) is a known value. The initial states for past inflows \({a_{t-1}^l}\), \(l = 1,\ldots,L\) are obtained through the generation of a set of \(L\) hydrological sequences for stages \(t=2,\ldots,T\). The inflow generation procedure is to randomly sample a vector of innovations \(\xi_t^l\) with a three-parameter Lognormal distribution and to calculate the inflow vector for stage \(t\), sequence \(l\) as:

\[a_t^l = \Phi_{t-1} \cdot a_{t-1}^l + \Lambda_t \cdot \xi_t^l \tag{A.3}\]

The matrices \(\Phi_{t-1}\) and \(\Lambda_t\) contain the stochastic inflow model parameters. \(\Phi_{t-1}\) represents the relationship between the inflows to a hydro plant in successive stages (time correlation).

In turn, \(\Lambda_t\) represents the relationship between the inflows to different hydro plants at the same stage (spatial correlation). For simplicity of presentation of the methodology a AR(1) model will be used. Note that the use of higher-order models does not affect the efficiency of the SDDP methodology.

Calculation of the Approximate Future Cost Function

The future cost function approximation is constructed with a recursion that goes backward in time. For each stage \(t\) and for each state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) the following process is repeated.

Generation of N conditioned inflow scenarios

The generation of \(N\) inflow scenarios conditioned to \(a_{t-1}^l\) is carried out as follows:

\[ a_t^{ln} = \Phi_{t-1} \cdot a_{t-1}^l + \Lambda_t \cdot \xi_t^n \tag{A.3} \\ \]

for each \(n = 1, \ldots, N\).

where \(\Phi_{t-1}\) and \(\Lambda_t\) are the stochastic inflow model parameters for stage \(t\) and the vector \(\xi_t^n\) results from a random sample of a Lognormal distribution.

Solution of the dispatch problem

Let \(v_{t-1}^l\) be the vector of initial storages and \(a_t^{ln}\) be one of the conditioned inflow vectors. The following operative problem is then solved for stage \(t\):

\[ \begin{array}{l l l l l l} & w_t^{ln} = \min z_t(e_t) + \alpha_{t+1} & & & \text{(A.5)} \\ \text{s.t.} & & & & \text{multiplier} \\ & v_t(i) + s_t(i) + u_t(i) \\ & \; \; - \sum\limits_{m \in M_i} (s_t(m) + u_{t}(m)) & = v_{t-1}^l(i) + a_t^{ln}(i) & & \pi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln}(i) \\ & \alpha_{t+1} - \sum\limits_{i=1}^I \Phi_{v_t}^p(i) \cdot v_t(i) & \geq \sum\limits_{i=1}^{I} \Phi_{a_t}^p(i) \cdot a_t^{ln}(i) + r_t^p &&\pi_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{ln}(p) \\ & v_t(i) \leq v_t(i) & & & \pi_{{\overline{v}_t}}^{ln}(i) \\ & u_t(i) \leq u_t(i) & & & \pi_{{\overline{u}_t}}^{ln}(i) \\ & e_t(i) = \rho_i u_t(i) & \\ % & & , j = 1, \ldots, J, p = 1, \ldots, P(t) \end{array} \]

where \(i\) indexes the hydro plants, \(p\) indexes the FCF linear cuts and where \(P(t)\) is the number of linear cuts to approximate the FCF in stage \( t \). Note that \(P(t)\) is initialized as zero.

Calculation of derivatives

After problem \(\text{(A.5)}\) is solved for each of the conditioned inflow scenarios, the derivatives of the objective function with respect to the initial conditions \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) are calculated.

The vector \(\frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial v_{t-1}^l}\) represents the variation of operating cost with respect to the initial storage. Because these storage values only appear in the water balance equation, one has:

\[ \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial v_{t-1}^l} = \pi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln} \tag{A.6} \]

where \(\pi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln}\) is the simplex multiplier associated to the water balance equation of problem \(\text{(A.5)}\).

The variation of operating cost with respect to the previous inflows, \(\frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l}\), is obtained as follows. Although \(a_{t-1}^l\) does not appear at the right-hand side of problem \(\text{(A.5)}\), the chain rule is used to obtain the derivative:

\[\begin{equation} \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l} = \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_t^{ln}} \cdot \frac{\partial a_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l} \tag{A.7} \end{equation}\]

Given that \(a_t^{ln}\) appears both in the water balance equation and in the future cost constraints, we have:

\[\begin{equation} \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_t^{ln}} = \pi_{v_{t-1}^{ln}}(i) + \sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \phi_{a_t}^p \cdot \pi_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{ln}(p) \tag{A.8} \end{equation}\]

The term \(\frac{\partial a_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l}\) is obtained replacing \(a_t^{ln}\) by expression \(\text{(A.4)}\) of the stochastic inflow model. Applying the derivatives results in:

\[\begin{equation} \frac{\partial a_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l} = \Phi_{t-1} \tag{A.9} \end{equation}\]

The desired derivative is calculated as the product of the last two expressions, that is:

\[\begin{equation} \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l} = [\pi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln} + \sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \phi_{a_t}^p \cdot \pi_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{ln}(p)] \cdot \Phi_{t-1} \tag{A.10} \end{equation}\]

For notational simplicity, we define:

\[\Phi_{a_{t-1}}^{ln} = \frac{\partial w_t^{ln}}{\partial a_{t-1}^l} \tag{A.11}\]

Calculation of the future cost function approximation

After solving the \(N\) problems corresponding to the \(N\) scenarios conditioned to state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) and calculating the derivatives with respect the initial conditions for each scenario \(n\), the expected value of those derivatives is given by:

\[\begin{align*} \Phi_{v_{t-1}}^l &= \frac{1}{N} \sum\limits_{n=1}^N \pi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln} \tag{A.12} \\ \Phi_{a_{t-1}}^l &= \frac{1}{N} \sum\limits_{n=1}^N \Phi_{v_{t-1}}^{ln} \tag{A.13} \end{align*}\]

and the expected value of the objective function is:

\[w_t^l = \frac{1}{N} \sum\limits_{n=1}^N w_t^{ln}\]

An approximation of the future cost function of the previous stage \(t-1\) is obtained through the linearization of the expected value \(w_t^l\) around the initial state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\):

\[ \begin{array}{l l l l} \alpha_{t-1}^{l}(v_{t-1}^{l}, a_{t-1}^{l}) & \geq & w_{t}^{l} + (v_{t-1} - v_{t-1}^{l}) \cdot \phi_{v_{t-1}}^l & \tag{A.15} \\ & & + \;\; (a_{t-1} - a_{t-1}^{l}) \cdot \phi_{a_{t-1}^l} \end{array} \]

Separating the known values from the decision variables y aggregating terms, we have:

\[ \alpha_{t-1}^{l}(v_{t-1}^{l}, a_{t-1}^{l}) \geq \phi_{v_{t-1}}^l \cdot v_{t-1} + \phi_{a_{t-1}}^l \cdot a_{t-1} + r_{t-1}^{l} \tag{A.16} \]

where \(r_{t-1}^l\) is a constant term given by:

\[ r_{t-1}^{l} = w_t^l - \phi_{v_{t-1}}^l \cdot v_{t-1}^{l} - \phi_{a_{t-1}}^{l} \cdot a_{t-1}^{l} \tag{A.17} \]

Updating the future cost function of the previous stage

The calculation of the FCF approximation produces a hyperplane that approximates the future cost function of the previous stage \(t − 1\) around the initial state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\). This process is repeated for each state \(l\), for \(l = 1,\ldots,L\). In this way we generate \(L\) approximations of the future cost function for stage \(t − 1\). These \(L\) new hyperplanes are added to the previous stage problem. Therefore, \(P(t − 1) ← P(t − 1) + L\).

Lower Bound Calculation

The operative problem is now solved for the first stage, \(t = 1\). The segments of the future cost function for this stage were obtained using the approximation method. The expected value of operating cost along the planning period is calculated as:

\[\underline{w} = \frac{1}{L} \sum\limits_{l=1}^L w_1^l \tag{A.18}\]

where:

Symbol Description
\(\underline{w}\) expected value of operating cost
\(w_1^l\) optimal value of the operating problem for the first stage given the initial volume \(v_0\) and the inflow vector \(a_1^l\)
\[ \begin{array}{l l l l l l} \tag{A.19} w_1^l & = & \min \sum\limits_{j=1}^J c_1(j) g_1(j) + c_{\delta}\delta_t + \alpha_1 \\ & & \text{subject to the operative constraints etc.} \end{array} \]

If the selection of the initial set of states and the calculation of the approximate FCF procedures are applied to all possible states \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\), the expected operating cost calculated in \(\text{(A.18)}\) would be, by definition, the optimal solution of the stochastic problem. Because the total number of states is too high, the procedure is applied to a subset of \(L\) states. Therefore, the calculated future cost functions \({\alpha_1}\) are approximations to the true functions. In particular, given that the future cost function approximation does not include all segments, the value \(\underline{w}\) in \(\text{(A.18)}\) is a lower bound to the optimal solution.

Upper Bound Calculation

The upper bound calculation is based on the observation that the expected cost resulting from an operative simulation to any future cost function cannot be lower than the optimal value.

The process consists of a simulation in the forward time direction for a sample of size \(L\). The simulation procedure is presented next.

Initial storage states

For stage \(t = 1\) we consider the initial volume vector \(v_0\).

Initial inflow states

The initial inflow states for stages \(t = 1,\ldots,T\) and for scenarios \(l = 1,\ldots,L\) are the same ones sampled in the Initial inflow states section.

Simulation of system operation

For each stage \(t\) and for each initial state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) the one-stage dispatch problem is solved:

\[ \begin{array}{l l l} \tag{A.20} & w_{t}^{l} = \min z_t(e_t) + \alpha_{t+1} \\ \text{s.t.} \\ & v_t(i) + s_t(i) + u_t(i) \\ & \; \; \; - \sum\limits_{m \in M_i} \left( s_t(m) + u_t(m) \right) = v_{t-1}^l(i) + \alpha_t^l(i) \\ & \alpha_{t+1} - \sum\limits_{i=1}^I \Phi_{v_t}^p(i) \cdot v_t(i) \geq \sum\limits_{i=1}^I \Phi_{a_t}^p(i) \cdot a_t(i) + r_t^p \\ & v_t(i) \leq \overline{v_t} (i) \\ & u_t(i) \leq \overline{u_t} (i) \\ & e_t(i) = \rho_{i} \cdot u_t(i) \\ % &\forall i \in [1,I]; \forall j \in [1,J]; \forall p \in [1,P(t)] \end{array} \]

where \(i\) indexes the hydro plants, \(I\) denotes the number of hydro plants, \(p\) indexes the FCF linear cuts and where \(P(t)\) is the number of linear cuts to approximate the FCF in stage \(t\) obtained in the recursive process.

The following value is associated to the solution of this problem:

\[ z_t^l = w_t^l - \alpha_t^l \tag{A.21} \]

where \(w_t^l\) is the optimal solution value and \(\alpha_t^l\) is the value of the future cost variable in the optimal solution. In other words, \(z_t^l\) represents the operating cost in stage \(t\), without the future cost.

Updating the initial storage state

For stages \(t\), \(t=2,\ldots,T\), update the initial storage states using the vector of final storage values \(v_{t-1}^l\) obtained from the solution of the dispatch problem of stage \(t-1\) for the \(l^{th}\) scenario.

Calculating the upper bound

After solving problem \(\text{(A.20)}\) for each initial state \((v_{t-1}^l, a_{t-1}^l)\) and for each stage, we calculate:

\[\overline{w} = \frac{1}{L} \sum\limits_{l=1}^L z^l \tag{A.22}\]

where \(z^l\) is the total operating cost of sequence \(l\):

\[z^l=\sum\limits_{t=1}^T z_t^l \tag{A.23}\]

Verification of Optimality

The upper bound estimated in \(\text{(A.22)}\) is based on a sample of \(L\) inflow sequences. Therefore, there is an uncertainty around that estimate, which depends on the standard deviation of the estimator:

\[\sigma_w = \sqrt{\frac{1}{L^2}\sum\limits_{l=1}^L(z^l-\overline{w})^2} \tag{A.24}\]

The 95% confidence interval for \(\overline{w}\) is:

\[[\overline{w}-1.96\sigma_w ; \hspace{0.2cm} \overline{w}+1.96\sigma_w] \tag{A.25}\]

If the lower bound \(\underline{w}\) is inside the interval \(\text{(A.25)}\), we have the optimal solution and the algorithm ends. Otherwise, we need to improve the approximation to the future cost by repeating the procedure presented in the approximation method section. The new storage states are the volumes \(v_{t-1}^l\) produced in the dispatch simulation presented in the upper bound calculation section. The inflow states \((a_{t-1}^l)\) remain the same.
